How hand pose is captured

These 18 methods cover every hand pose source in the 22 Taxel Atlas datasets, plus MediaPipe and HaWoR, which no catalog dataset uses yet but which are common ways to add pose to video. They are sorted on two axes: the hardware a recording needs, from any video to a worn glove or a robot, and how the pose is computed from the raw signal: a learned model, multi-view geometry, inertial fusion, a magnetic field, or a calibrated reading per joint.

Each entry draws what goes in and what comes out, with array shapes and units, and lists the published accuracy figures with the benchmark or reference system behind them.

    Reading the error numbers

    Each figure below comes from its own reference and conditions, so numbers compare within one method's list, and only loosely across methods.

      From a human hand to robot joints

      Teleoperated and human-to-robot datasets turn a tracked human hand into robot joint angles. The released robot joints are measured, but how well they match what the operator did depends on this mapping. The most common open tool is dex-retargeting from AnyTeleop (Qin et al., RSS 2023): it solves for joint angles that match human keypoints, in a vector mode suggested for teleoperation, a position mode suggested for offline human data, or a dexpilot mode that follows the DexPilot cost.
